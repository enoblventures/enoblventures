INSIDE EV SCORE

How the models help.
Where people matter.

Our proprietary EV Score helps us compare early-stage companies and focus our diligence. It brings structure to uncertain information. Our partners bring an understanding of founders, technology, and the world those companies operate in.

Models inform our decisions. People make them.

01 BUILD THE PICTURE

A company is more
than its pitch.

We bring together information about what a company builds, its founders, its financing history, and the markets it serves. We organize dates, funding rounds, and industry categories so companies can be compared more consistently.

This matters because startup data is incomplete and uneven. A missing funding record is not the same as no funding, and an estimated value is not an observed transaction.

Where judgment comes in. Our team asks founders what has changed, checks important gaps, and examines the context behind the data. A database cannot capture the whole business.

02 FIND COMPARABLE SIGNALS

Read the words.
Look for patterns.

Important information lives in company descriptions and founder backgrounds, as well as numbers. Natural-language processing helps turn that text into signals a model can compare across companies.

For example, two companies may describe similar products in very different language. Looking at meaning alongside business stage, sector, and financing history helps create a more useful comparison.

Where judgment comes in. A written profile cannot establish a founder’s judgment, integrity, or ability to adapt. Those questions require conversations, references, and an understanding of the people doing the work.

03 RANK THE OPPORTUNITIES

Different questions.
Several models.

The system uses multiple models to examine different questions, including the likelihood of subsequent funding and the occurrence of acquisitions or public listings. Models can account for financing stage, because a seed-stage company and a later-stage business are at different points in their development.

An ensemble combines information from several models. The resulting scores help us rank opportunities and focus deeper diligence on the top 25% by EV Score. A rank indicates relative priority, not a promise about what will happen.

Where judgment comes in. Our investment team evaluates the founders and their teams alongside the business, technology, and market before deciding whether to invest. We seek to understand how people work together, make decisions, and respond to challenges. A high score is not a guarantee of future performance.

Inside the modeling approach +

More than a decade of development and refinement.

Our proprietary modeling system reflects over 10 years of research, testing, and practical development in venture investing. We continue to refine it as new data emerges, technologies advance, and market conditions change.

Our modeling framework has used word embeddings to represent meaning in company descriptions and founder backgrounds, allowing comparisons beyond simple keyword matching.

Feature reduction methods, including PCA, PLS, UMAP-related approaches, and neural-network autoencoders, help turn complex information into more compact representations that models can work with.

Classification models examine the likelihood of events such as future funding or an exit. Regression models estimate numerical outcomes, including valuations. We evaluate different approaches and continue investing in research to improve how the models reflect real-world conditions.

These techniques help us identify patterns and sharpen our diligence. Our partners evaluate the people, challenge the assumptions, and consider changes in the world that historical data may not yet capture.

04 TEST AGAINST LATER OUTCOMES

Record the prediction.
Then check what happened.

We assess models on observations they did not learn from, known as out-of-sample testing. We also examine whether rankings recorded at an earlier date remain informative when later outcomes become available.

A longitudinal review followed 40,017 companies with rankings fixed in November 2022 through March 26, 2026. Higher funding and exit scores were associated with higher subsequent event rates in the broad cohort. In the 1,392-company YC-linked subset, the funding score identified a higher rate of subsequent financing.

The YC-linked results did not establish an exit advantage or validate the simulated portfolio return uplift. A funding event or acquisition is not the same as a profitable investment.

Study context: companies were operating and scored at the 2022 snapshot. YC links came from historical investor records, not a reconstruction of rounds available to Enobl. Results describe associations, not causation.

Where judgment comes in. We consider who was included, what outcomes were recorded, and how stage, timing, and missing data affect the result. Evidence of useful ranking helps our process; it does not prove a future return.

05 EXPLORE POSSIBLE OUTCOMES

Work with ranges.
Examine the assumptions.

Our broader modeling framework examines possible valuation ranges and portfolio outcomes. A valuation model estimates a distribution of values at a future financing event rather than one certain price. That is a different question from predicting an exit.

Monte Carlo simulation explores many possible portfolio paths under a set of assumptions. It helps examine how investment timing, portfolio breadth, follow-on capital, dilution, and rare large outcomes can affect results.

Where judgment comes in. Our investors challenge the assumptions against current conditions, deal terms, and the practical realities of access. Simulations are hypothetical, and useful company rankings do not by themselves validate a simulated fund return.

06 RESEARCH THAT CONTINUES

The world changes.
Our research continues.

We continuously invest in research and model improvement. That includes refreshing data, investigating errors, testing alternative approaches, and examining whether historical patterns remain relevant as technologies and financing conditions change.

Research also considers the wider economic environment. As models evolve, keeping earlier predictions fixed lets us distinguish what a prior model actually anticipated from what we learned afterward.

Where judgment comes in. Our team follows changes in customer behavior, competition, technology, and capital markets that historical data may not yet reflect. Updating a model is not enough; we must keep questioning how its outputs apply today.
